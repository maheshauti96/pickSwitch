VortexFlow — the spiral's middle · treatment options

Six ways to draw the hub, on the same content and the same palette the app actually uses. The constraint that matters: the spiral draws no plate, so wherever the hub stops being opaque, the caption's background becomes the desktop. That is the exact regression the palette work exists to prevent, so each option reports the measured contrast of its text against the surface at the caption's outer edge — not a guess, the same WCAG arithmetic the test suite uses. Anything marked fail is below 4.5:1 and cannot ship as drawn.

Recommended starting point: 5 · Caption-safe soft sun. It keeps a fully opaque surface beneath every caption line plus a 4pt safety margin, removes the rim, then fades continuously into a wide corona. This is the softest treatment that does not make wallpaper colour part of the text-contrast equation.

What to look for

  • Switch Desktop behind to Pale and Busy. Any option that fades out under the caption will fail there first, and a white wallpaper is common.
  • Turn on Show caption bounds. The dashed ring is where the outermost text sits; the fill has to still be opaque at that radius.
  • Push Watermark up. The current shipped value is 0.10 light / 0.06 dark, which is the measured ceiling for a flat disc — a treatment that keeps the centre brighter may afford more.
  • Try the Mono icon. A near-black logo is the worst case for a light theme and a near-white one for dark.

Numbers taken from the app

  • Hub diameter 192pt; caption column 156pt wide; title 15pt over up to three lines.
  • Light: white fill, text #201e1d, secondary at 68%, tint 15% toward the hue.
  • Dark: fill rgb(46,54,63), white text, secondary at 58%, tint 26% toward the hue.
