VortexFlow is for heavy macOS users — the ones with too many windows, not the ones with Mail and Safari. The preview is free and limited-time. You will create an account when you open the app. We do not ask for a credit card to start.

Get VortexFlow

Universal disk image, Apple Silicon and Intel. After it opens: create your account, then the preview begins.

What happens next

  1. Open the disk image and drag VortexFlow to your Applications folder.
  2. Open it. macOS will refuse, and say the developer cannot be verified. This is expected — keep going.
  3. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll to Security, and click Open Anyway next to VortexFlow. Authenticate when asked.
  4. Grant the permissions it asks for, then create an account. No credit card. Your limited-time free preview starts there.

You only fight Gatekeeper once. macOS then remembers VortexFlow as an exception, as described in Apple’s own documentation.

On macOS 15 and later, right-clicking the app and choosing Open no longer works as a shortcut for this. Apple removed that bypass.

Why it says the developer cannot be verified

VortexFlow is signed, but it is not notarized, because notarizing requires a paid Apple Developer account. macOS blocks unnotarized downloads. We would rather tell you this here than let Gatekeeper be the first voice you hear.
